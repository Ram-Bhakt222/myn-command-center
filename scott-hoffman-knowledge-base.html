Executive Summary This week

Key signals from the 2026-07-13 market scan. The macro thesis holds and sharpens: wellness is consolidating around functional, outcome-measured programming, and 2026 buyers increasingly demand proof-of-return before releasing budget. Three signals moved the needle this week — (1) hotel wellness now carries a hard revenue premium (properties command 20–30% ADR premiums and wellness travelers spend ~61% more per trip), (2) corporate wellness ROI benchmarks tightened hard (95% of programs that measure ROI report a positive return; up to $6 saved per $1 spent), and (3) California's CYBHI is now a $4B+ fee-schedule reimbursement pathway that turns school wellness into a sustainable, reimbursable line — a real catalyst that lifts the Schools lane.

Top 3 findings

  • Hotel wellness now has a hard revenue premium, not just a demand story. Wellness-positioned properties command 20–30% ADR premiums over conventional positioning, and wellness travelers spend as much as 61% more per trip — booking treatments, training, recovery circuits, curated dining and retail with longer dwell time and deeper loyalty. Total 2026 hotel guest spending is tracking to ~$805B (+1.7%). The buyer conversation has moved "from amenity to asset": wellness is now a core revenue strategy a director is measured on, not a nice-to-have. This is exactly the frame MYN's measured-outcomes programming is built to win.
  • Corporate ROI proof got sharper — and it favors the measurement seller. New 2026 benchmarks: 91% say wellness improves productivity, 87% say it lowers healthcare costs, 85% call it important for retention, and among orgs that actually measure program ROI, 95% report a positive return (up to $6 saved per $1). The gate is measurement, not goodwill — MYN's outcome layer is the unlock and the premium justification. Counter-pressure: Wellhub is compounding (partner payouts +107% globally, a new AI wellness coach for 2026, ~40k employers in-network) — but that's distribution of "access," not therapy outcomes.
  • Schools became fundable, not just sympathetic. California's CYBHI is a $4B+, five-year initiative whose fee-schedule reimbursement pathway lets districts bill for behavioral-health screening, treatment, and wellness programming — creating a sustainable funding source (schools/partners have already drawn $1.8M+ in new service funding, with BHSSA "Pathways to Student Wellness" grants layered on top). This converts the Schools lane from "budget-dependent" to "reimbursable," and is why Schools PMF rises this week.

Emerging formats & themes

  • Wellness as revenue, not amenity: the sharpest 2026 hospitality move — pilotable revenue plays (sleep-focused room category, recovery minibar, weekend wellness program) tested inside one quarter, backed by 20–30% ADR premiums and higher ancillary capture.
  • Functional over recreational: buyers want interventions that do something measurable (regulate stress, restore sleep), not activities that merely happen.
  • Accessible movement: walking yoga, chair yoga, adaptive yoga, and "Broga" have moved from niche to mainstream — the wellness industry is shifting away from high-intensity/discipline-heavy toward sustainable, nervous-system-supporting practice ("devotional" movement). Direct fit for non-yoga corporate/hotel/school populations.
  • Structured programs over single services: 5-, 7-, 14-day themed programs (stress reset, deep sleep) replacing one-off classes. Sell programs with a clear arc and a take-home plan.
  • Data-driven programming: hotels and employers using analytics on demographics/trip-purpose to avoid under- or over-programming — a measurement/data opening for MYN.
  • Community + recovery formats rising: community workouts and recovery-first modalities named as leading 2026 fitness trends alongside walking yoga.

Regulatory / structural

  • Yoga therapy mainstreaming for mental-health conditions and rapid expansion into schools and hospitals — reports frame this as a shift from niche activity to "globally mainstream health infrastructure."
  • CYBHI (California): $4B+ five-year initiative with a fee-schedule reimbursement pathway now lets K-12 LEAs bill for behavioral-health screening, treatment, and wellness — a structural, sustainable funding channel for the Schools lane.
  • AI-enabled personalization and guest/employee profiling raising expectations for tailored, evolving wellness journeys — reinforcing the case for a measurement/outcomes layer.

Pricing reality

  • Onsite group yoga benchmarks $150–$250 per class; aggressive group pricing as low as $20/person. Selling per-class is a race to the bottom — sell the program and the measured outcome instead.
  • Aggregators price on per-employee network access (from ~$11.99/mo consumer, PEPM enterprise). They monetize breadth; MYN monetizes depth + proof.

Competitive threats & gaps to watch

  • Wellhub is compounding, not just funded. +107% partner payouts, ~40k employers, and a 2026 AI wellness coach buy more distribution into the same HR buyer MYN wants. Counter by owning the therapy + measured-outcomes narrative their network can't credibly claim, and by anchoring in hotels.
  • Walking yoga remains unowned at the B2B brand level — no incumbent has claimed it as a B2B program despite the +2,414% surge. First-mover gap for MYN is still open.
  • Functional-wellness arms race in luxury hotels — groups building in-house cryotherapy, sleep, and nervous-system programming. Sell MYN as the partner that delivers measured therapeutic programming faster than they can staff it, at a 20–30% ADR-premium justification.
  • No dedicated IAYT-credentialed yoga-therapy B2B marketplace surfaced in this scan — the specialist-platform lane is still structurally empty.

Scott's Frameworks Consulting notes

Core operating frameworks from Scott Hoffman's consulting, applied to MYN.
  • Trojan Horse format: lead with the lowest-barrier offer (walking yoga) to clear the "my people won't do yoga" objection, then expand into deeper therapeutic programming once trust is established.
  • Dual-track marketing: Broad Tribe (IG/TikTok) generates reach and demand proof; B2B Authority (LinkedIn/YouTube) converts decision-makers. One funnel, two motions.
  • Outcomes over participation: sell measured results (sleep, anxiety, retention, attendance), not class counts — the only durable moat against $20–250/class commodity vendors, and the exact language CFOs require to approve budget.
  • Wellness as revenue, not amenity: position every hotel offer against the P&L (ADR premium, ancillary capture, dwell time) — matching the defining 2026 hospitality shift.
  • Fund-the-buyer: where a reimbursement/grant channel exists (CYBHI for schools), lead with how the program pays for itself — removes the budget objection entirely.
  • Anchor-first sequencing: win hotels as anchor references, run schools in parallel, take corporate selectively, keep individual as funnel leverage.
  • Authority compounding: every pilot becomes a case study becomes authority content becomes the next pilot — proof flywheel, closed with a two-peer-introduction ask.
  • "Space to think" standard: strip any subject line, claim, or CTA a serious medical reviewer would flag. Optimize for the buyer's decision quality, not vanity conversion.
