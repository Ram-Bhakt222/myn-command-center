Top 3 findings

  • The admin-rails players are buying their way into the program layer. CoreHealth Technologies — 25+ years, 4.5M+ individuals supported — acquired Konnected Workplace Wellness in August 2026 explicitly to add "expertise and operational support" to its technology, evolving from software vendor into a system-enabled wellness partner. This is the first hard evidence against MYN's "rails, not rivals" framing of the platform tier. It does not threaten the hotel anchor, but it compresses the Corporate lane: the platform now sells the bundle MYN sells piecemeal. Counter is credentialing depth (IAYT yoga therapy, not generic wellness coaching) and clinical citations, which an acquisition cannot manufacture quickly.
  • Two independent reimbursement doors opened for yoga therapy. Australia's federal government reinstated yoga as eligible for private health insurance rebates effective 1 July 2025 after a government-commissioned evidence review reversed the 2019 exclusion — Australia is already a named MYN priority market and ranks #2 globally on per-capita wellness spend ($4,218 vs. $831 global). In parallel, the American College of Physicians recommends yoga among first-line non-pharmacologic treatments for chronic low back pain, and yoga is now being prescribed in hospitals and clinics. Combined with California's CYBHI Fee Schedule (live since Jan 2026), MYN now has three distinct "the buyer does not pay out of pocket" channels. Healthcare moves from thin leverage to a credible fourth lane.
  • The US school funding picture is bifurcating — sell to allocation states, not grant cycles. A Healthy Schools Campaign survey of 1,440 district leaders found 80% expect reductions and layoffs of school health staff and 70% anticipate cuts to mental and behavioral health services; ~260 BSCA grantees across 49 states were notified of non-renewal. Simultaneously Wisconsin converted a $25M competitive grant into a $40M per-pupil allocation for FY2026 distributed to every district, and Massachusetts awarded $3.2M across 58 districts. The lane is not closing, it is concentrating: target states with per-pupil allocations or reimbursement pathways (CA/CYBHI, WI) and stop spending cycles on grant-funded districts. Schools PMF drops accordingly.

Emerging formats & themes

  • Longevity is the premium wedge in hotel wellness (NEW): the fastest-growing hotel-wellness segment is explicitly outcome-measured — VO2 max, body composition, personalized protocols — priced at a premium because results are measurable. This is the single strongest validation yet of MYN's outcomes-over-participation thesis, in the anchor lane.
  • Yoga therapy is crossing into prescribed clinical care (NEW): yoga is now being integrated directly into treatment plans and prescribed by physicians in hospitals and clinics, with the American College of Physicians recommending it among first-line non-pharmacologic treatments for chronic low back pain. The category is completing its move from alternative practice to evidence-based medicine.
  • Wellness is a hotel commercial pillar, not an amenity (reconfirmed, sharpened): hotels with a structured wellness proposition outperform comparable properties on TRevPAR, with the widest gaps at assets where wellness is a fully-fledged commercial pillar rather than a supporting feature. A property at EUR 80 RevPAR / EUR 120 TRevPAR is already drawing 33% of revenue from non-room sources.
  • Accessible movement keeps broadening (reconfirmed): walking yoga, chair yoga, gentle/hatha, breathwork and desk stretching continue moving from niche to mainstream. Walking yoga specifically removes every entry barrier — no membership, clothing, flexibility minimum or posture learning curve — and is being framed for urban professionals fitting movement into lunch breaks and commutes. Direct fit for non-yoga corporate, hotel and school populations.
  • Personalization, on-property activations and event-aligned programming top the 2026 hotel agenda (NEW): wellness in hospitality is no longer confined to properties with on-site spas — it is a holistic expectation touching every part of the stay, informed by neuroscience and biometrics. Most properties keep a core team of direct hires and use external partners for specialized roles, which is exactly the procurement shape MYN sells into.
  • Functional over recreational (retained): buyers want interventions that do something measurable (regulate stress, restore sleep), not activities that merely happen.

Regulatory / structural

  • Australia reinstated yoga for private health insurance rebates (NEW, major): effective 1 July 2025 the federal government reversed its 2019 exclusion after a government-commissioned review evaluated the evidence. Australia is a named MYN priority market and ranks #2 worldwide on per-capita wellness spend. This is a fund-the-buyer channel in an English-speaking priority market.
  • Certification is becoming the reimbursement gate (NEW): many healthcare providers and insurers require yoga therapists to hold certification in order to be reimbursed. Conditions most likely to qualify: back pain, neck pain, arthritis and joint stiffness, mobility limitation, and documented mental-health or chronic-stress conditions (anxiety, depression, PTSD). MYN's IAYT credentialing moves from a marketing claim to a billing prerequisite.
  • US school health funding is contracting (NEW, negative): a Healthy Schools Campaign survey of 1,440 district leaders found 80% expect reductions and layoffs of school health staff and 70% anticipate cuts to mental and behavioral health services; in April 2025 roughly 260 BSCA grantees across 49 states were notified their grants would not be renewed.

Districts currently spend between $4,000 and $11.6M annually on mental health services.
  • CYBHI (California, retained): Fee Schedule reimbursement pathway live since January 2026, claims retroactive up to 365 days, $400M Capacity Grants pool for LEA readiness ahead of 2026–27 family opt-in.

Pricing reality

  • Onsite group yoga benchmarks $150–$250 per class; aggressive group pricing as low as $20/person. Corporate yoga contracted B2B typically runs well above equivalent per-student public-class revenue — but selling per-class is still a race to the bottom. Sell the program and the measured outcome.
  • Aggregators price on per-employee network access (PEPM enterprise). They monetize breadth; MYN monetizes depth + proof.
  • Hotel spa economics give MYN its strongest pricing anchor: 28–42% operating margins and 6–14% of total hotel revenue mean a program that measurably lifts capture is priced against P&L contribution, not against a class rate.

Competitive threats & gaps to watch

  • NEW — platform-to-program consolidation is the defining risk this scan. CoreHealth acquiring Konnected is the template: buy delivery expertise, bundle it under existing platform contracts, and remove the need for a specialist vendor. Expect imitators. MYN's durable answer is the credential (IAYT) plus clinical evidence (ACP first-line recommendation, hospital prescribing, Australian rebate eligibility) — assets that take years, not one acquisition, to assemble.
  • NEW — class vendors are marketing hotel and resort yoga directly. At least one provider now advertises certified teachers for hotel, resort and event yoga. The anchor lane is uncontested at the program level but no longer at the teacher-supply level. Keep every hotel proposal framed as a multi-day measured program with a take-home plan, never as staffing.
  • Wellhub keeps compounding. $605M raised, $2.4B valuation, 15,000+ companies, ~40,000 employers, partner payouts +107%. The reach gap in Corporate widens every quarter. Compete on measured therapeutic outcomes, never on network size.
  • Walking yoga remains unowned at the B2B brand level. Still the 2nd-fastest-growing 2026 fitness trend at +2,400% search growth, explicitly framed for beginners, older adults and injury recovery — i.e. the exact populations hotels, schools and employers actually have. First-mover gap still open.
  • No dedicated IAYT-credentialed yoga-therapy B2B marketplace surfaced this scan. The specialist-platform lane remains structurally empty — but CoreHealth's move is a reminder that "empty" and "defensible" are not the same thing. Speed matters.

Scott's Frameworks Consulting notes

Core operating frameworks from Scott Hoffman's consulting, applied to MYN.
  • Trojan Horse format: lead with the lowest-barrier offer (walking yoga) to clear the "my people won't do yoga" objection, then expand into deeper therapeutic programming once trust is established.
  • Dual-track marketing: Broad Tribe (IG/TikTok) generates reach and demand proof; B2B Authority (LinkedIn/YouTube) converts decision-makers. One funnel, two motions.
  • Outcomes over participation: sell measured results (sleep, anxiety, retention, attendance), not class counts — the only durable moat against $20–250/class commodity vendors, and the exact language CFOs require to approve budget.
  • Wellness as revenue, not amenity: position every hotel offer against the P&L (ADR premium, ancillary capture, dwell time) — matching the defining 2026 hospitality shift.
  • Fund-the-buyer: where a reimbursement/grant channel exists (CYBHI for schools), lead with how the program pays for itself — removes the budget objection entirely.
  • Anchor-first sequencing: win hotels as anchor references, run schools in parallel, take corporate selectively, keep individual as funnel leverage.
  • Authority compounding: every pilot becomes a case study becomes authority content becomes the next pilot — proof flywheel, closed with a two-peer-introduction ask.
  • "Space to think" standard: strip any subject line, claim, or CTA a serious medical reviewer would flag. Optimize for the buyer's decision quality, not vanity conversion.
